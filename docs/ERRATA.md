# Errata

## 2026-11-06: evidence frames and visual-check crops could show a later frame than the detector's

**What was wrong.** The detector analyses the n-th frame of the video, read one after another, and records it at n ÷ fps seconds (`compact_vision.py`, `vision.py`, `device_pipeline.py`). The app then cut each evidence frame and each visual-check crop from the video again with an ffmpeg seek to that time (`ffmpeg -ss <time> -i …`: `server.frame_image`, and the frozen `look.crop_frame` that the visual check used). That seek does not always return the detector's frame:

- **SDG-Warehouse clips (our bundled samples and test footage).** They are fragmented MP4s with one fragment per frame and a real keyframe every 30 frames, but every frame is flagged as a sync sample. ffmpeg therefore seeks straight to the requested frame, the decoder cannot decode it on its own, and the first image comes from the next real keyframe: a detection on frame 215 got the image of frame 240, up to 29 frames (0.97 s) late. Frames that are themselves keyframes (0, 30, 60, …) came out right.
- **Other files.** The time was rounded to milliseconds, so on many files it fell just after the frame and the next frame was returned (one frame late, for example on every third frame at 30 fps); the last frame of a file could give no image at all; and on a variable-frame-rate file the frame shown at n ÷ fps seconds is not the detector's frame n.

The detector, its boxes and every detection score were never affected: they come from the decoded frames.

**Fix.** `media.frame_at` now writes the frame the detector analysed: frame number round(time × fps), counted from the first frame in decoding order, exactly as the detector counts. In the first 30 s of a file it decodes from the start and picks the frame by number. Later in a file it looks up the n-th frame's own timestamp in the file's packets, seeks to 10 s before it and keeps the frame with exactly that timestamp, and it uses that result only if decoding began at a real keyframe at or before it; otherwise it decodes from the start and counts. Every evidence frame and every visual-check crop of the CPU and UGen300 paths comes from it (`media.crop_at` for the crop, with the frozen crop region, scaling and JPEG quality). `look.crop_frame` is frozen with the Stage I record and is kept only to reproduce it; the app no longer calls it. On a variable-frame-rate file the image is still the detector's frame n, and the time shown stays the detector's n ÷ fps, which can differ from that frame's own timestamp. Tests on numbered synthetic clips (plain, SDG-style fragmented, variable-frame-rate and dropped-frame MP4) check every path: `tests/test_frame_exact.py`.

**Published numbers.** No published number changes.

- **Stage I visual check** (<!-- fact: look.tp_confirmed -->104 of 106 annotation-matched pairs confirmed, <!-- fact: look.agreement_both -->144 of 148 crops agreeing with the visual labels, and the other figures in `data/evaluation/look/`): every test frame was sampled at 1 frame per second from 30 fps clips, so every one was a multiple of 30, a real keyframe of these clips, where the seek lands correctly. We cut all test crops again from the intended frame, chosen by number: <!-- fact: frame.stage1_identical -->148 of 148 are byte-identical to the published crops. Re-running the frozen look-v2 check on them through the app's resident CPU worker, with 6 threads instead of the published 12, gave the same answer for <!-- fact: frame.stage1_rerun_same -->148 of 148 crops, and the same raw reply for every one. The corrected numbers are therefore the published ones: <!-- fact: look.tp_confirmed -->104 of 106 confirmed, <!-- fact: look.fp_flagged -->1 of 7 gold-FP crops flagged, <!-- fact: look.agreement_both -->144 of 148 agreeing with the visual labels. Records: [data/evaluation/stage2/frame-timing](../data/evaluation/stage2/frame-timing/README.md).
- **Detector results** (pair precision and recall, events found, the UGen300 HEF comparison) and all timings: not affected; no image cut by the seek was used.
- **The app on the bundled samples and the screenshots:** they sample 1 frame per second, so the evidence frames were already the detector's; an end-to-end job on `warehouse-1` gives the same images before and after the fix. At 5 frames per second, a sensitivity setting, <!-- fact: frame.sample_5fps_late -->3 of 4 evidence frames and crops were late before the fix and 4 show the detector's frame now; the late ones were 6 to 24 frames behind.
- Our unpublished Stage II crop studies sampled other frames of the same clips and were affected; no number from them is published here.
